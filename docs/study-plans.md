# Study plans for Core 1 and Core 2

Three plans: a 7-day plan to use once per core in the final week before each exam, a 2-day plan for when an exam is close, and a longer timeline for the whole certification. All of them start with the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) so you know where your marks are leaking.

A word of honesty first, as our guidance: seven days is enough to **finish** preparing for one core, not to start from nothing. If most of the topics on the domain pages are new to you, stretch each day of the 7-day plan into a week of study, and keep the order. The plans follow the weights: the heaviest domains get the most time.

## 7-day plan

Use it once for Core 1 and once for Core 2. Expect 1.5 to 3 hours a day.

### Core 1 (220-1201) week

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Read the [Core 1 overview](core-1/README.md). Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) under time. Note your two weakest Core 1 domains, weighted by how much each is worth. |
| 2 | Domain 3 (25%) | Read [Hardware](core-1/3-hardware.md) in full. Do both items. Write RAID levels, RAM types and connector names from memory. |
| 3 | Domain 2 (23%) | Read [Networking](core-1/2-networking.md). Do both items. Write the port table from memory twice, once in the morning and once at night. |
| 4 | Domain 5 (28%) | Read [Hardware and Network Troubleshooting](core-1/5-hardware-and-network-troubleshooting.md). Do both items. Build a one-page symptom and likely cause table. |
| 5 | Domains 1 and 4 (13% and 11%), first timed run | Read [Mobile Devices](core-1/1-mobile-devices.md) and [Virtualization and Cloud Computing](core-1/4-virtualization-and-cloud-computing.md) and do the practice item in each. Then take a full timed practice test, such as the [free CompTIA A+ practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/a-plus?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=a-plus). Score it by domain. |
| 6 | Fix the weakest heavy domain | Go back to the weakest domain from day 5. Reread its "Traps that cost points" section and redo its items without the answers. |
| 7 | Second timed run and rest | Take a second timed test in the morning. Compare domain scores with day 5. Read the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). Stop studying by early evening. |

### Core 2 (220-1202) week

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|

| 2 | Domain 1 (28%) | Read [Operating Systems](core-2/1-operating-systems.md) in full. Do both items. Open each Windows tool and run each command-line tool in the objectives once. |
| 3 | Domain 2 (28%) | Read [Security](core-2/2-security.md). Do both items. Write the malware removal steps and the wireless security protocols from memory. |
| 4 | Domain 3 (23%) | Read [Software Troubleshooting](core-2/3-software-troubleshooting.md). Do both items. Build a symptom and fix table for Windows, mobile and security issues. |
| 5 | Domain 4 (21%), first timed run | Read [Operational Procedures](core-2/4-operational-procedures.md) and do its 2 items. Then take a full timed practice test, such as the [free CompTIA A+ practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/a-plus?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=a-plus). Score it by domain. |
| 6 | Fix the weakest heavy domain | Go back to the weakest domain from day 5. Reread its "Traps that cost points" section and redo its items without the answers. |
| 7 | Second timed run and rest | Take a second timed test in the morning. Compare domain scores with day 5. Read the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). Stop studying by early evening. |

### How to review a missed item

Most of your improvement comes from reviewing misses properly. For each one, answer three questions:

1. **What did I confuse?** For example: SODIMM vs DIMM, IMAP vs POP3, Type 1 vs Type 2 hypervisor, gpupdate vs gpresult.
2. **Which word in the stem should have stopped me?** For example: "FIRST", "Windows Home", "without losing data", "most likely".
3. **What will I check next time?** One short habit, such as "check the edition before choosing a Windows feature".

Keep the answers in one running list. By day 6, the same few confusions will keep appearing, and fixing those is worth more than doing another hundred new questions.

## 2-day plan

For when one exam is already booked and close. Use the domain pages for the core you are sitting.

### Day 1: find and fix the big gaps

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 min | Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) for your core. |
| 2 | 40 min | Read "Key concepts" in the heaviest domain: [Core 1, Domain 5](core-1/5-hardware-and-network-troubleshooting.md#key-concepts-explained), or [Core 2, Domain 1](core-2/1-operating-systems.md#key-concepts-explained) and [Core 2, Domain 2](core-2/2-security.md#key-concepts-explained). |
| 3 | 45 min | Read "Traps that cost points" on every domain page for your core, then do the practice items for your two weakest domains. Read every explanation. |
| 4 | 60 to 90 min | Take a full timed practice test, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/a-plus?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=a-plus). |
| 5 | 30 min | Review every miss with the three questions above. |

### Day 2: sit the exam

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 min | Redo yesterday's misses without the answers. |
| 2 | 10 min | Read [performance-based questions and pacing](performance-based-questions.md) and the [exam-day checklist](exam-day-checklist.md). |
| 3 | 15 min | A light warm-up: write the port table (Core 1) or the malware removal steps (Core 2) from memory, then 10 mixed items. Stop if you feel tired. |
| 4 | Exam | Sit the exam rested and on time. |

## A longer timeline for the whole certification

Our guidance for someone starting A+ with little or no IT experience, studying about an hour a day:

| Weeks | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 to 6 | Core 1 content, one domain at a time in the order on the [Core 1 overview](core-1/README.md#how-to-study-core-1). Do each domain's items as you finish it. |
| 7 | The Core 1 7-day plan above, then sit Core 1. |
| 8 to 13 | Core 2 content, in the order on the [Core 2 overview](core-2/README.md#how-to-study-core-2). |
| 14 | The Core 2 7-day plan above, then sit Core 2. |

That is roughly three to four months in total. With IT support experience, many people can compress each content block to two or three weeks. If you prefer Core 2 first, swap the blocks; see [which core to take first](a-plus-overview.md#which-core-to-take-first).

## What not to do

- **Do not use brain dumps.** CompTIA's authorized materials policy, printed in both objectives documents, says people who use "brain dump" material (leaked real exam questions) will have their certifications revoked and be suspended from future testing. Beyond the risk, they teach answers to someone else's questions, not the reasoning you need for yours.
- **Do not mix versions.** Core 1 and Core 2 must come from the same version. Study the V15 objectives for 220-1201 and 220-1202, not an older series.
- **Do not skip the small domains.** Mobile Devices and Virtualization and Cloud Computing are only 13% and 11% of Core 1, but they are full of quick definition marks.
- **Do not cram late the night before.** Tired readers miss words like FIRST, NOT and BEST, which is where many marks go.
